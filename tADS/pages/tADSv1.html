Introduction

“Dark Star is always playing somewhere.” Jerry said that. “We just tap into it.” It’s one of those hippie phrases that gets tossed around every once in a while on the internet, like an ad for something you just bought. But I think it's true. Every Dark Star is a half-hour dip in a flowing stream, a ballgame you listen to while you’re driving home. It exists, regardless of you listening to it, or of the Grateful Dead playing it. Every Dark Star is a point on a line.

I’ve decided I want to write about a few select performances that I really like, partly as a journaling exercise, but partly also because a really, really good Grateful Dead improv piece has so much richness to it. So much life! So many worlds to explore. You can get really properly lost. Like a blind man in a cave. I’m not sure how much my annotations can really add, honestly. Oh well. Nothing else to do but start.

This first one is one of my favorites. You know the story. Hottest day in Oregon history. Folks complain about the guitars being out of tune, and they're sort of right. Sun sets on a dusty fairground.

Pictured above, from left to right: Garcia, Weir, Kreutzmann.

For the uninitiated, the musicians are as follows:

- Jerry Garcia: lead guitar (panned left)

- Bobby Weir: rhythm guitar (panned center)

- Phil Lesh: bass

- Keith Godchaux: piano (panned right)

- Billy Kreutzmann: drums

Dark Star 8/27/72

A large portion of this show was filmed and you can find it on Youtube, if you're interested. (It's where I got the audio file from.) Personally, I think it ruins the magic a little.

Pretty typical intro. Jerry and Phil poking around.

1:31: Some really neat distortion on Phil’s bass as he runs up to the sixth and backs off.

2:02: I like the piano chord here. Keith and Bobby decide on E minor momentarily for the next ten seconds or so.

2:30: Main theme has sort of broken down. A quieter place by 3:00. Drums are staying on top of things, though.
